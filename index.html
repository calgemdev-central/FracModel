Bottomhole: closure stress + net pressure + perforation friction + near-wellbore friction. Surface: bottomhole − hydrostatic + pipe friction. The bars update with the cursor.

Bottomhole pressure (left axis) and G·dP/dG (right axis) after shut-in. Normal leakoff gives a straight G·dP/dG line through the origin; closure is where it bends away.

    Each step: friction = STP + hydrostatic − pipe friction − ISIP (bottomhole). Fit: perforation friction ∝ q², near-wellbore ∝ √q.

    Nolte–Smith log-log plot during pumping. Small positive slope (about 1/8 to 1/4): confined height, normal extension. Flat: height growth or fissure opening. Slope near 1: restricted tip, screenout coming.

    The bi-wing fracture inside a layered rock block: shale barriers (higher σh) above and below the pay sand. Width is colored by aperture: widest (red) at the wellbore at mid-height, thinning to the tip and to the top and bottom edges, the PKN shape w = wmax(1 − x/L)¼√(1 − (z/h½)²). "Color: propped" shows where proppant holds the fracture open; the pad beyond the dashed propped length closes after shut-in. The inset is the vertical width profile at the wellbore. Height is drawn at a stretched vertical scale (shown on the view) and aperture is exaggerated so both are visible; the real values are in the readout. Drag to rotate; play or scrub to watch it grow.

      Training model, not a design tool. Fracture geometry uses a PKN length–width relation (Economides & Nolte form) with constant fluid efficiency and material balance; breakdown uses the Hubbert–Willis/Kirsch relation for a non-penetrating fluid, which cased and perforated wells often depart from. Shut-in decline follows Nolte's G-function for Carter leakoff, with closure where η = Gc/(2 + Gc). The wellbore column is tracked as plug flow. Pipe friction is Fanning/Blasius for water, reduced by the friction-reducer percentage. Annulus response and leak, screenout and water hammer are illustrative.
